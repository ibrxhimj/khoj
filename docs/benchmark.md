Generated by `python scripts/benchmark.py` (seed 2026). The benchmark builds its own graph (3696 nodes, 5147 edges): the demo dataset plus 200 held-out projects, 100 with hidden risk (25 per pattern, weak and strong variants) and 100 clean controls with confounders. **Every held-out project has a clean surface**: registered, approvals granted, A-khata land, no complaints, no delay.

A project counts as flagged when its score is at least 30 (MEDIUM or HIGH).

**How to read this**

- *Surface check* is what a buyer sees on a RERA portal or brochure: the project's own complaints, approvals and land. On these projects it finds nothing, by construction. That is the point: the risk is only in the relationships.
- *No structural rules* lets paths pass through other projects and chain land-ownership edges. Watch its false positives: the confounders (shared aggregator, shared locality) start to look risky.
- *Strongest path per flag* drops path summation, so two shared directors count the same as one.
- Misses are reported, not hidden. The weak variants are hard on purpose: a contractor late on a single project, or a title suit naming only the previous owner, sit near the threshold.

**Cypher vs pure-Python reference:** every score was recomputed by enumerating paths in Python. 0 mismatches across 200 projects (max |ΔK| = 0.0).

**Latency** of a full risk trace on this graph: p50 2.9 ms, p95 4.4 ms.

**Disclosure of a change made after the first run.** The first run applied distance decay to every hop. Land recall was 52%: a title suit naming only the plot's previous owner scored 26.5, just under the threshold. We changed the model so title-chain hops (`PREVIOUSLY_OWNED_BY`) carry their weight but no distance decay, because a buyer inherits the vendor's title. Since that change was made after seeing this benchmark, treat the land-pattern result as in-sample. First run, for comparison: AUC 0.969, recall 77%, land recall 52%. The first run also had a benchmark bug, since fixed: the late projects in the contractor pattern were attached to companies from the pool used for clean controls, which caused the 8% false positives.

Limits: the data is synthetic and the patterns were designed by us, so this shows the model does what it claims on known patterns, not how it performs on real filings.
